Why most ERP projects run over budget

Three planning mistakes we keep seeing, and how to avoid them before you sign.

FRFarhana RahmanPrincipal Consultant12 Oct 20266 min read

After 40 ERP implementations, the same three causes explain nearly every overrun. None of them are technical, which is why a bigger budget rarely fixes them.

1Scope is agreed before processes are mapped

Teams sign a fixed scope, then discover that each department runs the same process differently. Change requests start in week three and never stop. Map the real workflows first, then price the work.

A fixed price on an unmapped process is just a guess with a signature.

2Data migration is treated as a final step

Dirty product and customer records surface late and block testing. Start cleaning data in the first month, and assign an owner in each department.

Key takeaway

Migration problems cost ten times more to fix in testing than in the first month. Treat data as a workstream, not a task.

3Nobody owns adoption

Training is scheduled for the week before launch. Name a champion per team early and let them test every release, so the first time staff see the system is not on go-live day.

Before you sign, check that

  • Every department has walked through its real workflow
  • Data owners are named, and cleaning has started
  • A champion per team will test each release
  • Go-live support is budgeted, not assumed

4What to do next

Pick one department and map its workflow this week. If the map surprises you, the budget is wrong, and it is far cheaper to learn that now.
